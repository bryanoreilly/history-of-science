## Topics Covered

- The Age of Exploration as a condition for early modern knowledge-making.
- The Columbian Exchange as ecological, biological, economic, and intellectual transformation.
- Plants, animals, diseases, people, and ideas moving between the Americas, Europe, Africa, and Asia.

- New World plants and European botanical classification.
- Carl Linnaeus and sexual systems of plant classification.
- Colonial botany, commercial exploration, the Providence Island Company, and Indigenous ecological knowledge.
- Scientific collecting, cabinets of curiosity, and early museums.
- Alfred W. Crosby's role in naming and framing the Columbian Exchange.

## Summary

Episode 16 widens the history of science beyond European laboratories, observatories, and universities. The Columbian Exchange made early modern knowledge global by moving organisms, people, diseases, crops, technologies, and information across oceans. This was not a neutral circulation of facts: it was bound to conquest, forced labor, ecological disruption, corporate profit, Indigenous expertise, and imperial violence.

The episode also shows how new encounters with unfamiliar plants and animals reshaped European ideas about nature. Botanical collecting, comparison, classification, and museums grew out of this global exchange. Linnaeus's later classification system belongs to this longer process of turning an overwhelming flood of specimens into ordered knowledge.

The central lesson is that early modern science cannot be separated from global contact. New knowledge emerged from ships, plantations, trading companies, colonial violence, Indigenous knowledge, gardens, cabinets of curiosity, and commercial networks as much as from books and instruments.

## Important Dates

- 1400s-1600s: The Age of Exploration intensifies long-distance maritime contact and imperial expansion.
- 1492: Columbus's first voyage opens sustained post-1492 exchange between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia.
- 1500s: Old World diseases, crops, animals, and people transform American societies and environments.
- 1633: The Providence Island Company sends English colonists to the western Caribbean with commercial and botanical aims.
- 1707: Carl Linnaeus is born.
- 1735: Linnaeus publishes the first edition of `Systema Naturae`.
- 1753: Linnaeus publishes `Species Plantarum`.
- 1972: Alfred W. Crosby publishes `The Columbian Exchange`.
